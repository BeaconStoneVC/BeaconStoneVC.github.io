Methodology

BeaconStoneVC records what venture capital firms and their portfolio companies publish on their own public pages, and the changes between daily checks. We check those pages against other sources too: news reports, and for people, their own LinkedIn profiles.

Our standard

We follow what a firm's or company's own website says, unless there's evidence to the contrary. A change that isn't correct should never appear here, so anything we couldn't check, or that outside evidence contradicts, is held back and looked at again the next day. It may appear late or not at all.

In November 2026 we discarded everything we had previously published and started again from nothing, because we could no longer tell which of our old records were right. We add firms as fast as we can keep to this standard, so coverage is small and growing.

We read pages one at a time. Our browser says what it is and who we are ("BeaconStoneVC"), we do what each site's robots.txt asks, and we never try to get past a site that refuses us. LinkedIn is the one exception to the first two: to see whether someone's own profile says they have left, we read it slowly through a logged-in account, and our browser there doesn't say who we are. If LinkedIn refuses us, we stop and stay away for a week.

How a list is checked

  1. Each page is opened in a real browser and saved.
  2. A small program written for that page reads the saved copy and produces a list. Before that program is first trusted, and whenever it changes, an AI agent with its own browser goes through the page as a visitor would and writes down what it finds, without seeing the program's list.
  3. Both are compared with the last full check. If neither the page nor the list has changed, what was proven then still holds, and nothing more is needed.
  4. If either has changed, or a week has passed, AI readers go through the saved page piece by piece without seeing the list, and write down what they see.
  5. The two are compared. Any real difference holds that page back, and we keep showing its last proven list and the date it was proven.

How a change is checked

A first check of a page only records where it starts, so it never counts as a change. After that, each change is examined on its own:

  • A company newly listed must be confirmed on the firm's page by two separate AI readers, quoting words that a program then finds on the page.
  • A company no longer listed must be gone from the firm's page on two separate days. We say "no longer listed" because that is what the page proves. It doesn't tell us that an investment ended.

How we test the checks

Every day we plant errors, such as a made-up name on a saved page, a false departure, or the wrong website for a company. They go through the same checks as real data. If any is missed, nothing is published that day.

How often we're wrong

Every day, AI readers also try to prove wrong a sample of our claims, picked at random from everything we hold: each company on a firm's page with the details we show, each person on a team page, each website and each change. Because the picks are random, what they find measures how often we're wrong. It covers what we show, not what we've missed: a company left off a list is what the daily reading of each page looks for. Since 10 November 2026 they settled 50 checks and proved 0 wrong. So we can say with 95% confidence that no more than 5.9% of our claims are wrong.

People

We never publish anyone's name, job title or where they moved to. People changes appear only as daily counts by region, and only where we track at least 10 firms, because a smaller count could point to one firm. Someone counts as having joined or left when the team page says so, unless a news report or their profile shows otherwise. If their own LinkedIn says they have left, we take that over the team page, and use the date it gives. If it still shows them there, we wait a few weeks before following the team page, because people update their profiles late. This section may be empty for some time.

Dates

The date beside a change is when we detected it. Where two AI readers find the same date in a source, and a program confirms the quoted words, we also show when it was reported to have happened.

Corrections

We keep checking after publishing. Every day an AI reader tries to prove some of our claims wrong, starting with the ones checked longest ago, alongside the random sample described above. If two readers find evidence that holds up, we take the change down and record it on our corrections page.

Companies and websites

A company's stage, status, location, sectors, year and description are shown exactly as the firm's page states them, attributed to that firm, and checked the same way as its name. We leave them blank rather than fill them in from anywhere else. When a firm's page changes a company's stage or status, that counts as a change.

When two firms list a company under different names, we treat them as one company only if two AI readers each find so independently and a program confirms the quoted evidence. Otherwise we keep them apart. A website link appears only after an AI reader has opened the site and confirmed it belongs to that company.

Limits

This is a record of what public pages say, and those pages can themselves be wrong or out of date. These checks make errors rare but can't rule them out. Provided as is, with no warranty. Not investment advice. Please verify against primary sources before relying on anything here.
